
This week [[Mabel]] and I picked new rituals to write/draw out, dissect, and rearrange.

I particularly struggled with my own ritual because I made it more broad and vague than I thought it was. Specifically, my ritual was a student walking into class and finding their seat in an attempt to gather the focus needed for the upcoming lecture.

As I was writing out the different stages in a rough journey map, I kept finding more and more divergent branches about the different paths and contexts that could be coming into play for this student.

In an attempt to address every context, it wasn't enough to supplement the fact that it was simply too broad to be reconstructed into something new. I felt like I was writing out the routes for a choice-based game (something I made my junior year of high school).

Thankfully, [[Professor Shah]] picked up on this and encouraged me to make it more specific, so I did by making it about myself.

When I'm walking to class I blast music to block out my thoughts, when I walk in I keep it playing but I lower it, and when I finally sit down and I go from audio to visual by doomscrolling on my phone until class starts. Prior to doomscrolling though I take out my class materials first so I'm ready to just put my phone down and pay attention.

We played around with this a bit by changing the context and reversing the order of the steps I take (ex: class ended and now you have to head straight to a party).

With [[Mabel]]'s end of month journal ritual her example was explicit and simple to question/discuss. Her visuals made the thought process of rearranging better to understand and it made me think that maybe I should break out of the habit about writing down words and connecting everything with arrows (it gets visually crowded very quickly).

With that said, [[Mabel]]'s ritual was able to be very transformative. We went from her writing about her month privately to a group of grieving individuals with a shared traumatic experience looking for a way to express their emotions and thoughts.

I loved getting to question how the meaning of her ritual could be changed, exercises like these help me become inspired because they nudge me to think differently.

It makes me excited to do more UX work because this kind of activity shows that there are many possibilities to transform processes/rituals we thought could otherwise not be. This means there is potential, hopefully in what I want to pursue in my career as well.
