[[Professor Shah]] also explained to me the difference between an archetype and a persona.

A persona, the standard UX example, is a specific individual with a face, name, etc. made to represent just one of the people within the audience of an experience. 

An archetype on the other hand is more general. They still hold traits however they aren't tied to the image of only one person, they're more holistic. 

For example, a persona is James who is a college freshman working at the library 3 times a week. An archetype is a college freshman who works part-time.

For our issue, we had decided that an archetype would be more fitting. Our archetype is someone, like me, who holds themselves to high expectations when it comes to productivity and accomplishment.

The goal however is to address that initial feeling of guilt I get when something is not done when I want it to be. We don't expect to rid of this guilt entirely however we want to minimize it drastically.

For the next steps, we highlighted two points throughout this storyboard that I could attempt to address:

2. When I shut my eyes from the sound of the alarm

I decided to first try before the first visual with the computer. This is because I noticed that there is an end to this experience with reflection but there really isn't a defined beginning.

My idea is that throughout the next week I will do a quick reflection on what I want to accomplish during the time period I designated for work and look back on it when the alarm goes off.

Therefore I can properly reflect on what I did and did not get done. While this might sound demeaning, [[Professor Shah]] and I both agree that reflection helps with reducing guilt. It also means that these beginning reflections aren't meant to be strict, they're simply just a way for me to keep track of my thoughts and expectations. This also means I'm not overwhelmed with having to reflect everything at once when it is time for class.
